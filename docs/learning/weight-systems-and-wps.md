# Weight systems, simplex circuits, and weighted projective spaces

This note distinguishes barycentric weights, integer Newton-hull IP and weighted projective spaces (WPS). Equal weights need not determine the same lattice polytope. The supporting formal declarations below retain their precise hypotheses; the complete executable reflexive classification is described in [the statement guide](../../proofs/STATEMENTS.md).

## 1. Three normalizations that must remain distinct

For an IP d-simplex with lattice vertices v0,…,vd, the unique barycentric coordinates of 0 satisfy

`q_i>0, Σq_i=1, Σq_i v_i=0`.

They are rational because they solve an invertible rational affine system. Clear denominators and divide the common gcd to obtain a **primitive integral relation** `Σw_i v_i=0` with positive integers w_i and gcd(w_i)=1. Conversely q_i=w_i/D, D=Σw_i. Thus normalized rational weights mean sum one in KS notation, whereas primitive integral weights mean global gcd one. Neither phrase by itself means well formed.

Conrads [C02], Definition 2.2, pp. 217–218, uses different terminology:

| Condition                             | Meaning here                | Conrads' terminology                              |
| ------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Σq_i=1 for positive rational q        | Barycentric normalization   | Not his definition of normalized integral weights |
| gcd(w0,…,wd)=1                        | Primitive integral relation | Reduced                                           |
| gcd(w0,…,omit w_i,…,wd)=1 for every i | Well-formed WPS weights     | Normalized                                        |

Example: (2,3,4) is globally primitive, but omitting 3 leaves gcd(2,4)=2. Conrads' Example 3.9, p. 223, replaces it by the well-formed tuple (1,3,2), with the same WPS fan. Dividing a _common_ gcd changes only scale; well-forming a tuple may change individual entries and therefore the barycentric relation of a particular chosen simplex. The coarse WPS can remain isomorphic even when the original integral simplex changes. One must preserve the original relation and lattice data when using weights as polytope invariants.

Conrads' Definition 2.3, p. 218, assigns the **determinant weight** `Q_i=|det(v0,…,omit v_i,…,vd)|`. Its factor λ=gcd(Q_i) is the index of the vertex-generated lattice in the ambient lattice (Lemma 2.4). The primitive relation is `Q_red=Q/λ`, and the simplex type is `(Q_red,λ)`. This Q is not automatically the primitive relation. The barycentric coordinates are `Q_i/ΣQ_j=Q_red,i/ΣQ_red,j`.

For an affinely independent family of m lattice vectors in a larger ambient space with a positive relation, the same rational positive circuit exists in the family span; intrinsic interior replaces ambient interior. The circuit specifies one relation. A CWS requires an independent family of such relations plus support coverage, not arbitrary repeated rows.

## 2. The WPS and the lattice realization

Conrads [C02], §3, p. 218, defines `P(w)=Proj C[X0,…,Xd]`, graded by deg Xi=w_i. Theorem 3.6, p. 221, constructs a fan with d+1 rays and an integral simplex of type `(Q_red,1)` spanning that fan. Corollary 4.6, p. 224, makes this factor-one simplex unique up to unimodular equivalence. Corollary 3.7, p. 222, says its vertices are primitive precisely when the reduced integral weight tuple is well formed; passing to primitive ray generators produces the well-formed tuple and preserves the fan. Corollary 3.8 identifies WPS isomorphism classes by the well-formed weights, with entry permutations understood.

A complete simplicial fan with d+1 **primitive** rays whose positive relation has weights w defines the WPS P(w) when the rays generate the ambient lattice. Buczyńska [Bu08], Notation 5.1 and Proposition 5.4, §5, explicitly requires those primitive rays and lattice generation. A larger ambient lattice can carry the same rational fan and same positive weights while producing a finite quotient of that WPS. This is a **fake WPS** in her Definition 6.1. Lemma 6.3 and Theorem 6.4, §6, realize the quotient via the inclusion of the vertex/ray-generated rank-d lattice N' into N; the WPS on N' is the unique universal cover in codimension one. The primitive-ray hypothesis ensures no branching in codimension one. This conclusion is stronger than a generic finite toric quotient and must not be asserted when a nonprimitive vertex is being used as a ray generator.

Conrads' Proposition 4.7, p. 224, also shows that the toric variety associated with any integral simplex is a finite-group quotient of P(Q), with quotient group abstractly isomorphic to `Z^d/N_Δ`. The distinction is that his simplex definition permits nonprimitive vertices: its generated sublattice is not always the lattice generated by **primitive ray generators**. That proposition alone does not assert a cover unramified in codimension one. When the vertices are primitive, the two lattice constructions coincide. For a reflexive lattice simplex the vertices are primitive: a nonprimitive multiple kv, k>1, cannot pair to ±1 with an integral polar vertex on a facet through kv.

For a well-formed primitive relation w, one can express the WPS lattice as the free quotient `Z^(d+1)/Z w`. Rational circuit data recover the real relation space, but an integral simplex can embed its vertex-generated lattice with nontrivial finite index in another ambient lattice. Real-linear equivalence and a forward integral map are consequently weaker than a lattice isomorphism. The primitive weights alone do not enumerate all reflexive simplex lattice realizations, let alone all CWS realizations.

## 3. What Conrads proves about reflexive simplices

Conrads uses the polar convention `<y,x>≤1` in equation (2.1), p. 216. The frozen package and KS use `<y,x>≥−1`. The polars differ by negation, so dual integrality and reflexivity agree; signs of displayed facet normals must be translated.

Theorem 4.4, pp. 223–224, states that every integral d-simplex of type `(Q_red,λ)` is unimodularly equivalent to `H Δ_Q`, for H in the finite set of determinant-λ Hermite normal forms. Definition 4.1 and Theorem 4.2 fix that matrix set. Remark 4.5 warns that different H may give isomorphic simplices: generating these matrices is coverage, not canonical deduplication.

Proposition 5.1, pp. 224–225, has two directions with different hypotheses. If an integral simplex is reflexive, each entry of its reduced weight divides the total reduced weight. Conversely, when an integral weight tuple satisfies this divisibility, the unique **factor-one** simplex associated to its reduced tuple is reflexive. For a well-formed tuple w, this gives the familiar WPS Gorenstein criterion `w_i | D` for every i, D=Σw_i. The relation argument is explicit: an integral polar vertex for the facet opposite v_i pairs with v_i by `1−D/w_i` in Conrads' sign convention, so D/w_i must be integral. For factor one this divisibility is sufficient; for a larger ambient lattice, the same rational polar may cease to be integral.

There is a source hypothesis qualification that should be retained. Corollary 5.2, p. 225, prints the WPS criterion without restating well-formedness. Read as a criterion for _arbitrary_ presentations of the same WPS it fails: Example 3.9 identifies P(2,3,4) with P(1,3,2). The second has total 6 divisible by each entry, but the first has total 9 not divisible by 2 or 4. Apply the criterion to the well-formed presentation. Similarly, the proof of Proposition 5.1 says the divisible tuple is “necessarily normalized”; that needs prior gcd reduction (the scaled tuple (2,2,2) satisfies divisibility but is not well formed as written). The simplex construction is scale invariant, so the factor-one conclusion survives after reduction. These qualifications come from checking the source's own definitions and examples, not from silently changing its terminology.

Lemma 5.3, p. 225, shows that dual reflexive simplices have the same reduced weights, possibly different factors. For the factor-one simplex Δ_Q let m_Q be the factor of its dual (Definition 5.4). Proposition 5.5 proves that the factor λ of any reflexive simplex with these reduced weights divides m_Q. This is a necessary restriction. The algorithm on p. 226 still tests whether `(H^T)^(-1) Δ_Q*` is integral, then compares isomorphism classes. Divisibility of λ alone does not assert that every determinant-λ H gives a reflexive simplex. The classification algorithm is for simplices and lattice realizations; it does not prove WS/CWS subpolytope coverage of all reflexive polytopes or native-pipeline correctness.

## 4. Simplex IP and integer Newton-hull IP are different tests

For positive rational q with sum one, write

`Q_exp(q)={a_i≥0 : Σq_i a_i=1}`, `Δ_Newton(q)=conv(Q_exp(q)∩Z^(d+1))`.

The all-ones vector belongs to the exponent equation. The **IPWS condition** asks whether it lies in the intrinsic interior of the full integer Newton hull. It is stronger than positivity of q in an abstract simplex circuit. For well-formed weights with all w_i dividing D, the exponent simplex has integer vertices `(D/w_i)e_i`; its integer hull is the entire exponent simplex, and 1 is in its intrinsic interior. When divisibility fails, the integer hull may still have IP, but it requires an independent test.

An original exact example separates the predicates: take weights (1,1,3), D=5. The lattice triangle with vertices `(-1,-3),(1,0),(0,1)` has positive relation with those weights, so 0 is interior. But integer exponent solutions of `a0+a1+3a2=5`, ai≥0 have a2≤1; 1 has a2=1 on a supporting boundary, so the Newton hull is not IP. Positive normalized weights can therefore describe an IP simplex while failing IPWS.

Conversely, an IPWS need not yield a reflexive Newton hull in dimension five: the exact example `(1,1,1,1,1,2)/7` and its primitive distance-two facet are derived in [the polarity/pyramid note](ip-reflexivity-and-pyramids.md#an-actual-nonreflexive-ip-weight-system-an-independent-derivation). It is a one-point integer Newton hull, without a Gorenstein WPS weight tuple. Skarke's low-dimensional theorem concerns the integer hull in dimension at most four, not all simplex circuits or arbitrary ambient-lattice realizations.

The converse connection relevant to KS requires a geometric witness. If a positive simplex circuit is formed from **dual lattice points of a reflexive polytope P**, the pairing map sends integral generators of P into the depth-one weight lattice, and surjectivity over the reals sends an open neighborhood of 0 onto an open neighborhood in the weight hyperplane. Its image lies in the Newton hull, proving IPWS. No claim that the pairing map is onto the **integer** weight lattice is needed for that interior argument.

## 5. Two examples about lattice and projection hypotheses

### Same weights, distinct lattice polytopes

The triangle `T=conv((1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1))` has reduced weights (1,1,1), factor one, and area 3/2. Apply the integer matrix `H=((1,1),(0,3))`, of determinant three. The result has vertices `(1,0),(1,3),(-2,-3)`, reduced weights still (1,1,1), factor three, and area 9/2. All three vertices are primitive. Its facet inequalities are

`x≤1`, `x−y≤1`, `−2x+y≤1`.

Their normals are integral, so the triangle is reflexive (negate normals for the package convention). Its fan in the generated index-three sublattice gives P²; in the ambient Z² it gives a fake WPS finite quotient of P². The two triangles are not unimodularly equivalent because their lattice areas differ. This is an independently derived example; no census or formal-certificate claim is made. It explains why simplex weights alone cannot distinguish even reflexive lattice polytopes.

### An abstract CWS can lose block projection surjectivity

Take two rows `(1,1)` and `(1,2)` in R². They are nonnegative, linearly independent, and cover both coordinates, hence meet the frozen abstract CWS structure. Their joint kernel is {0}; the hull of its allowed lattice points is {0}, with 0 interior in this zero-dimensional subspace, so abstract CWS IP holds. The second row's kernel is the line `y1+2y2=0`; its depth-one integer generators are `(0,0)` and `(2,−1)`. Their hull has 0 as an endpoint, so that row fails IPWS. Projection from the joint zero space is not surjective onto the row's line. This elementary example explains the explicit lifting hypothesis in `cws_ip_component`. The geometric simplex-block theorem below proves its own surjectivity under stronger geometric hypotheses.

The pairing-surjectivity proof compares real dimensions using the single relation kernel and matrix rank under transpose. The block-IP proof restricts the pairing map to the weight hyperplane, uses it as an open surjective real-linear map, and uses integral pairings only in the forward direction. Thus it avoids confusing real surjectivity with an integral isomorphism.

## 7. Obligations and computational implications

| Required connection                                        | Evidence available                                                                 | Gap or consequence                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Real simplex weights → rational primitive integral weights | Lattice vertices and rational affine-system argument; Conrads determinant weights  | `simplex_weights` alone is real; `KS/RationalWeights` and `KS/CircuitWeights` supply the rational/primitive bridge        |
| Primitive relation → well-formed WPS presentation          | [C02] Definitions 2.2–2.3, Corollary 3.7                                           | Preserve simplex data when passing to primitive fan rays; gcd one alone is insufficient                                   |
| WPS weights → all reflexive simplex realizations           | [C02] Theorem 4.4, Proposition 5.5 and dual-integrality test                       | `KS/Realizations` reconstructs compatible lattices; WPS-specific toric interpretation and exact normal forms are separate |
| Positive circuit → Newton-hull IP                          | Fails for (1,1,3)/5; geometric block theorem supplies sufficient witness           | Native weight acceptance must test the integer hull, not positivity alone                                                 |
| IPWS → reflexive Newton hull                               | Low-dimensional Skarke theorem; explicit 5D failure                                | Separate IP/reflexive predicates and counts in dimension five                                                             |
| CWS IP → component IP                                      | Frozen `cws_ip_component` with hlift; geometric circuits supply a different bridge | Arbitrary abstract rows do not guarantee projection surjectivity                                                          |
| Abstract reachability → executable WS/CWS search           | `KS/WeightSearch` and `KS/Assembly` prove terminating sound/complete enumeration   | Native input pools, descriptors and pruning need separate refinement; this is not a production census                     |
| Source's WPS Gorenstein criterion                          | Divisibility on a well-formed presentation                                         | Conrads Corollary 5.2 needs that presentation qualification; do not copy its shorthand to arbitrary reduced tuples        |

Equality of weights is not equality of lattice polytopes. The concrete Lean classifier includes compatible lattice realizations and subpolytope search, while native refinement and exact normal-form deduplication remain separate guarantees.
